# Awesome System One Models [![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

A list of System One models, a class of AI models built to make fast, structured decisions (also called decision models). Jev from TypeSafe was the first. This list covers it, the open and hosted alternatives, and the agents, tools, SDKs and benchmarks built around them.

A System One model doesn't write text. You hand it some state and a few typed questions, and it answers with a choice, a score, or a yes/no probability. There's nothing to parse, and it's fast and cheap enough to sit in front of routing, classification, verification and agent supervision. A sibling class is forming next to the decision models — extraction models, which answer with typed values pulled or predicted from the input instead of choices. The wider System 1 / System 2 literature — cascades, routing, dual-process agents — is lineage rather than membership, and lives under Reading. The name comes from Kahneman's System 1.

Prefer a web version? Visit [system-one-models.com](https://system-one-models.com) to search and sort the list, with stars and growth for each entry.

## Models

Trained for the job — state in, typed answers out. Hosted or open weights.

### Open weights

- [agent-jev](https://github.com/malevrigns/agent-jev) - AgentJev-0.6B: a small trained decision model that takes unstructured state (diffs, traces, logs) and returns calibrated distributions in one ~50 ms forward pass. Apache 2.0.
- [Ainvest-Decision-27b](https://huggingface.co/Ainvest-World/Ainvest-Decision-27b-v1.0) - Text-only decision model on Qwen3.8-27B scoring options through a custom 255-way readout with last-token pooling and noncausal full-attention layers — evaluated as an M02 weight merge, 26B stored params in BF16. #15 on the Decision Index's Nov 6 draw (68.23); the card is precise about what its numbers are ("existing public-run scores, not a maintainer-assigned Full score"), ships publication validation (zero choice flips on a fixed 100-request sample), and credits the [tasksource corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/tasksource/tasksource-jev-typed-decisions) (2.55M rows with per-row license fields — the class's largest public training set) for much of the gain. Prompts over 16,384 tokens per question are refused, never truncated. Apache 2.0 with the full inference package in-repo.
- [Arcbright Elf 1](https://huggingface.co/arcbright/elf-1-12b) - Indie Gemma-4 fine-tune family (12B and 31B) sitting top-5 on JevBench's official composite (77.9 on the Nov 17 draw) with a published [reproduction dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arcbright/elf-decision-index), serving code in-repo, 54.5 ms one-pass bf16, and a 40,960-token hard refusal — never truncates. The Jeffan pattern again: the board placement is the story. Apache 2.0.
- [basal-omni-1.0-ultra](https://huggingface.co/Remek/basal-omni-1.0-ultra) - Polish–English decision family on Gemma 4 (31B ultra, 12B max) with its own [basal engine](https://github.com/rkinas/basal): up to 255 options, `choice` / `noul` / `score` plus `tool` (picks the tool from OpenAI or MCP definitions, not the arguments), `act`, and `multi`, on `/v1/systemone`. Trained on ~640K PL/EN decisions distilled from basal-1.5, shipping per-type temperatures and certified confidence thresholds in the weights — the card publishes coverage at 1% target error, an option-order flag, an FP8 build, and zero-shot images through Gemma's encoder with honest probes of where image answers break. JevBench's October board ranks ultra #4 official among 192 open systems (71.7, above the Jev 1.13 reference). Apache 2.0.
- [bekko-system-one](https://github.com/hotchpotch/bekko-system-one) - Yuichi Tateno's (hotchpotch) tiny Japanese entrants: 17M/68M/400M choice/noul/score + reranking models with shared-prefix attention, a Sentence-Transformers training toolkit, ONNX inference in Node and the browser (CPU/WebGPU), and a public training dataset — from one of Japan's best-known OSS authors, with an honest S1MB-vs-Jev comparison. MIT, ~43 stars; HF [release post](https://huggingface.co/blog/hotchpotch/bekko-system-one-v0-release).
- [Beveridge](https://huggingface.co/sathiskumarjothi0/Beveridge-Large-C) - The small-and-local entrant: 398.2M parameters (ModernBERT-large plus a 3.5M decision network), English, 4,096-token inputs, running on a laptop CPU — "nothing to parse and nothing to hallucinate," documents never leaving your machines. Three variants (Large-C, Pro-C, Pro-C-Web) with the `bev-decisions` PyPI package, and a first-party Beveridge-Bench of operations documents where Large-C posts 93.0 against Jev's 87.9 (author-reported). Apache 2.0.
- [Bobcat Flash 1.2](https://huggingface.co/sanghwa-na/bobcat-flash-1.2) - foxl.ai's Gemma-4-26B-A4B build (two merged rank-64 LoRAs) with one of the two most rigorous cards in the class: 98,368-token decision contexts with zero failures and a published long-context degradation table (−2.3 pt at 92K vs Flash 1.1's −16.0), wrong-answer-injection resistance (8.2% vs the base's 31.1%), a sealed-final eval with a pre-declared release bar, and English+Korean. Top-16 official on JevBench's Nov 17 draw after topping v1.6.1's open board. Apache 2.0, [write-up](https://foxl.ai/blog/bobcat-typed-decisions).
- [Bonsai-JEV](https://www.modelscope.cn/models/dakepeng/Bonsai-JEV) - The JEV recipe on Prism ML's Ternary Bonsai 2 27B (2.13-bit ternary Qwen3.8): a vision-capable 27B decision model running in ~7 GB — KL 0.0163 vs JEV-27B's 0.017, AgentRewardBench AUROC 0.930 vs 0.91, visual public 93% of JEV-27B-VL, with MLX/Vulkan/Jetson paths and an honest limitations section. The quantized-local corner nobody else occupies.
- [chinese-decision-unified](https://www.modelscope.cn/models/quincyqiang/chinese-decision-unified-4b) - One weight, two readouts: Chinese-native `noul`/`choice`/`score` letter-logit decisions AND typed span extraction (NER/relation/event) in the same forward pass (~65 ms on an A800) — 0.8B/2B/4B on Qwen3.5 over a 13M-row CJ-Dataset, with CJ-Bench (307,900 items; the 4B at 86.86%) and span micro-F1 68.9. The first Chinese-native instance of the decision-plus-extraction sibling pattern this list's intro describes. [GitHub](https://github.com/yanqiangmiffy/chinese-decision).
- [Chon-1.0-12B](https://huggingface.co/ninhnv/chon-1.0-12b) - Vietnamese–English LoRA (~1 GB) on an unmodified Gemma-4-12B-it: `choice` (2–16 options), yes/no, and `score`, served on the TypeSafe wire by the [chon](https://github.com/xoai/chon) package. The card is honest that most decisions are one pass but some take a short extra pass (p95 23.5 s) — and on JevBench's Nov 3 re-measure that honesty aged badly: the 12B fell from #26 to #168 on the official composite (14.2), while the family's new [Chon-1.1-31B](https://huggingface.co/ninhnv/chon-1.1-31b) now leads it at #23 on the Decision Index's Nov 6 draw (67.54). Own 720-item held-out replica published. Apache 2.0.
- [Clef](https://huggingface.co/Cloudflare/clef) - Cloudflare's open decision-model family, Apache 2.0: frozen Qwen backbones with a trained routing head, prefill-only non-autoregressive scoring. Clef (27B) and Clef-flash (9B) take text + image with a 64K context; [Clef-omni](https://huggingface.co/Cloudflare/clef-omni) (Qwen3-Omni 30B-A3B MoE) adds native audio and video — a 21 s clip with sound scores in ~1.5 s, text in ~130 ms. Clef's October runs put it first on the Jev Decision Index for a time; on the Nov 6 draw it sits #89 (54.92) with the open fine-tune wave well past it, while Jev and Laya still lead some tasks. Hosted on Workers AI — Clef-flash at $0.038/M input undercuts Jev — with an RL fine-tuning platform, in production on Threat Intelligence.
- [CLM](https://github.com/Contrastive-LM/CLM) - Contrastive Language Model: dual state/action encoders (frozen Qwen3-8B + 20M heads) where candidates embed once and cache, rather than re-reading the state. Apache 2.0, TypeSafe wire-compatible. Claims Jev parity on computer-use, gaming, and tool-calling at up to 9× lower latency, plus SOTA held-out verifier results (Terminal-Bench 2.1, DeepSWE).
- [Copernicus](https://huggingface.co/Poindexter-Labs/Copernicus-31B) - Poindexter Labs' merge house: the 31B is a weight merge of KnowLine-31B-Gen1 + Sieve-31B-v3 (ATTRIBUTION.md in repo), the 57B a two-part Gemma+Qwen ensemble inspired by REAX — together they took #6 and #7 on the Decision Index's Nov 19 draw, and the 57B tied #1 on public-only items. The ensembling-the-open-wave pattern REAX started, now run by a third-party lab. Apache 2.0.
- [Cua-S1](https://github.com/trycua/cua/tree/main/libs/cua-s1) - Research stack for small specialist computer-use models. First checkpoint is form-oriented: [cua-s1-forms](https://huggingface.co/cua-ai/cua-s1-forms), a 706K-param option scorer with its [training dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/cua-ai/cua-s1-forms) on Hugging Face. Planning and execution stay separate.
- [d1](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-open) - Liquid AI's open decision-model family: d1-3B (text + vision, from LFM2.5-VL), d1-omni-600M (adds audio), and a late-October [d1-2B](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/d1-2B) on LFM3-VL. Single forward pass; on the Decision Index's October draw d1-3B scored 48.57 on the public split — best under 10B that week — at ~8 ms on an RTX 4090 (the family is absent from the Nov 6 board). Open weights and GGUF builds on Hugging Face, served natively by upstream vLLM. The hosted side grew past d1-ultra on [Nov 16](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-2): d1.2 and d1.2-ultra add audio input and five new output types — typed extraction with verbatim spans, "not found" abstention instead of guessing, find-all spans, and box localization — at $0.02 / $0.042 per M input, with each request billing its text and images once no matter how many questions ride them (OpenRouter and Vercel "coming soon"). d1.2-ultra tops Artificial Analysis's Decision Index v2.1 at 75 — the first entrant to displace Drex Max.
- [d3](https://huggingface.co/vllm-sr/d3-ultra) - The vLLM-SR org's flagship line (promoted from the Vela entry — the two are different products): d3-ultra (27B) and siblings span text, JSON, images, and video with choice, noul, ordinal, `box` grounding, and `track` outputs — the interface extended into visual grounding and object tracking. Multiple JevBench top-20 regulars (d3-turbo #6 capability) and top JDI placements (d3-ultra #7, d3-turbo #25 on the Nov 6 draw). Six checkpoints (d3, -flash, -mini, -nano, -lite, -edge; 27B down to 0.6B) now serve natively on stock vLLM as classification models — d3-ultra and -turbo still need the org's own fork — and note the runtime trade: d3's bidirectional attention means prefix caching and chunked prefill are off. Apache 2.0.
- [decider](https://github.com/Mapika/decider) - Qwen3.5 fine-tunes (0.8B up to a 35B MoE), a Gemma-4-12B variant whose v2 ranks #27 on JevBench's official open-weights composite (73.7), and training-free readouts of larger chat models; Apache 2.0 weights on Hugging Face. Serves TypeSafe `/v1/systemone`, so the official SDK works by repointing `TYPESAFE_BASE_URL`.
- [Decisio](https://opendecisio.com) - Open decision family on Gemma 4 (12B and 31B, v0.8 / v0.9): its v1.6.1-era peak — #3 of 192 on JevBench's official composite — did not survive the re-measures (Nov 3: the 12B at #59 of 235, the 35B-A3B at #137). The line's second act is the [tuned-commit family](https://huggingface.co/tachara-ai/Decisio-12b-tuned-commit-think) (Apache 2.0, tachara-ai): same weights, three decision_config variants, with confidence-gated choice-only thinking — top-p < 0.3712 triggers ≤256 tokens of reasoning on 8.95% of items at unchanged 60 ms median — and unusually honest stats: +4.95 on the threshold-chosen set ("optimistic"), +0.75 on untouched natural wording. Official #11 on JevBench's Nov 17 draw. Answers 50 questions on a 3K-token doc in ~1.1 s by evaluating questions in batch. 58.55 on Decision Index 0.3.2.
- [dgem](https://github.com/GoogleCloudPlatform/dgem) - Google Cloud Platform's open entrant: zero-shot decision model on DiffusionGemma — policy templates answered in one diffusion pass, a hesitation score for abstention, and a CLI + MCP server + gateway + Studio deployment story. From a major org, no training required.
- [dohnuts](https://github.com/PsiACE/dohnuts) - PsiACE's small multimodal decision models: choose, judge, or score over a message, document, or image in one forward pass, with multiple questions sharing the same input. The Python toolkit covers training, inference, eval, and agent integration; ships a 0.8B model for a consumer GPU. Apache 2.0.
- [Drex](https://github.com/nace-ai/drex-decision-models) - Nace.AI's decision models, now with the class's second foundation paper ([Drex: Towards Foundation Decision Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07854), Nov 5: RLAF plus consistency losses across four open models, 8B–27B). v2.0 runs on a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B MoE with a Kev pointer head and claims 60.9% head-to-head against Jev in their eight-game arena; the Max line (27B multimodal, 262K context, FP8, local deploys down to Apple Silicon) tops Artificial Analysis's Decision Index v2.1 (Nov 11) — the first Nace claim an independent board has confirmed — and Max Finance tunes a 35B MoE for invoices and receipts. Weights carry Nace's Open RAIL-M license (not Apache; check commercial terms); the API is $0.05/M with a 131K context, Max at $0.031/1k (#7 on JevBench's v1.8 API composite, 75.4), everything also on OpenRouter.
- [DuplexJev](https://github.com/adventists-ai/duplexjev) - Speech decision models for full-duplex voice agents (32B and 4B): typed single-token readouts judge turn-taking and dialogue state in one pass, served on vLLM — one of the first dedicated audio entrants AudioJevBench hasn't measured yet. ~146 stars.
- [FlexDM-LoRA 2.1](https://huggingface.co/1000luca/FlexDM-LoRA-v2.1) - The adapter-is-enough entry: a 4 GB LoRA (r128, the exact average of two seeds, applied at strength 0.7) on an unchanged Gemma-4-31B-it, served unmerged in vLLM over FP8 — no full fine-tune, no custom runtime. The reader lists every question before the state, reads short requests twice, and re-reads low-confidence answers with options reversed; the readout is the option-label logits at `Answer: (`. #4 on the Decision Index's Nov 6 draw (71.61, listed in the #2 tie group), with the highest new-domain score of any single model (79.92 — only the REAX ensemble is higher) and +4.05 Full over the same reader untrained (#22); the card publishes the 34-task private breakdown, calibration (77.9% accuracy, ECE 0.054, Brier 0.305), and full decontamination. CC BY-SA 4.0.
- [FRIDA-Decisions](https://github.com/ai-forever/FRIDA-Decisions) - SberAI's Russian-first encoder decision model: `choice` / `score` / `noul` plus a `ranking` question type, all questions scored in one packed encoder pass with an exact state cache. Torch, int8 ONNX, MLX, or vLLM.
- [GEV-26B-Decide](https://huggingface.co/autotrust/GEV-26B-Decide) - AutoTrust's decision-model line on Gemma 4 — and the org's traction actually lives in its [Decider-Pro family](https://huggingface.co/collections/autotrust/autotrust-decider-pro-6898ec1f3c1a2b4c1d0f5202): dual-head (system-one plus system-two) builds whose Vision-27B and 35B each top a million downloads and five thousand likes, trained on the 741K-row [jev-distill-corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SargeDev/jev-distill-corpus-v3), with a paper (arXiv 2609.14973) and demo Spaces — the most-downloaded open family nobody had listed. GEV-26B-Decide itself is the adaptive-thinking entry: System 1 answers in one ~45 ms pass, and below 0.8 confidence the unmodified base thinks in Gemma's thinking mode with its distribution folded 50/50 into the result. Apache 2.0, vLLM, ~1.1M downloads.
- [GLiNER2.5-Decide](https://huggingface.co/fastino/GLiNER2.5-Decide) - Decision specialist from the GLiNER team: any label set at call time — intent, routing, multi-label tags, ordinal scores — answered in one forward pass with no generated tokens. Apache 2.0, in 340M / 1B / multilingual builds. Claims 60.2% vs JevK5's 57.6% on Fastino's own 17-domain suite; on JevBench's harder multi-step items it scored near chance — a 340M classifier built for simpler task shapes.
- [GUI-Decisions](https://github.com/infinitylogesh/GUI-Decisions) - GUI grounding as a decision instead of generation: a fixed slot template (action, x, y, x2, y2, ...) read from next-token distributions, with coordinates as 256-bin labels rather than decoded JSON digits. ~150 ms per computer-use step on Gemma 4 31B, about 3x faster than the same model writing JSON. LoRA training code plus 2B and 31B weights on Hugging Face.
- [H2O-Lightning-4B](https://huggingface.co/h2oai/h2o-lightning-4b) - H2O.ai's multimodal decision family: the 4B on Qwen3.5 (text plus record images, one pass per decision, runs on unmodified vLLM behind a stdlib shim, Apache 2.0) and the [31B](https://huggingface.co/h2oai/h2o-lightning-31b) on Gemma-4-31B-it (v1.3.0, FP8, one-engine overlay). On JevBench's Nov 17 board the 4B v1.1 is #3 official (77.9) and the 31B #4 on capability — and the 31B now leads ImageJevBench capability at 80.9; note its license turned gated-custom in November (the 4B stays Apache). The family has stayed top-five through every reshuffle since topping the v1.6.1 composite. Live ZeroGPU demo on HF Spaces.
- [hs-decide-1-flash](https://huggingface.co/hindsight-ai/hs-decide-1-flash) - Hindsight AI's flash decision model — a new org's first release, ~1.3K downloads and 23 likes in three weeks. Watch the family.
- [imajev](https://huggingface.co/mohit67890/imajev-4b) - The Qwen3.5 vision-LoRA family ImageJevBench credits with leading open weights — 2B/4B/9B plus GGUF mirrors, ~112 likes on the 4B. Finally linked.
- [Intern-Decision](https://huggingface.co/collections/internlm/intern-decision) - Shanghai AI Lab's multimodal decision family (internlm org): 0.8B / 2B / 4B on Qwen3.5, returning an answer distribution per question in one forward pass over text and images. Apache 2.0, with a [GitHub training stack](https://github.com/InternLM/Intern-Decision), an HF Spaces demo, and a community CoreML port.
- [iota-decide-4b](https://huggingface.co/macrocosm-os/iota-decide-4b-v0.4) - Macrocosmos AI's entrant (the Bittensor-infrastructure company): Qwen3.5-4B with bidirectional full-attention LoRA, trained via streaming DiLoCo across 16 consumer RTX A4000s over the open internet — no cluster — with an honest 8-gram contamination audit. JevBench official top-15 (77.3 Nov 17 draw), ~33 ms/decision, 8.4 GB. Apache 2.0.
- [Jebadiah v2.1](https://huggingface.co/frontier-infra/jebadiah-27b) - Frontier Infra's LoRA pair — 27B on Qwen3.8, 9B on Qwen3.5 — assigning a probability directly to each option. Apache 2.0 with GGUF and MLX builds, and unusually honest reporting: Decision Index 0.4.1 puts the 27B (T1e recipe) at 64.4, tied 6th, and the 9B at 50.9 at 125 ms median latency — while the author notes the gains over v2 are not shown significant and When2Call declined. The 9B is the calibration pick.
- [Jeeves](https://github.com/PostHog/jeeves) - PostHog's open 9B (Qwen3.5-9B + LoRA + pointer head): answers directly at ≥0.9 confidence, otherwise reasons first — with a block-4 diffusion drafter for the thinking pass and two fitted temperatures for the two modes. Beats Jev on JevBench's public hard set (0.820 vs 0.730) and out-of-domain vs Kev-9B/JevK5; ~20 ms no-think, 1.2 s median think on H100 FP8; runs on 48 GB Apple Silicon; `jeeves_sdk` is a typesafe-sdk drop-in. MIT code, Apache weights, 471 stars and a 242-point HN thread; independently benchmarked by Arize (which also found its rewording fragility — 0.85→0.25 AUC). The reason-when-unsure lane with the most traction.
- [Jeff-1.1](https://huggingface.co/jgeuter/Jeff-1.1-Qwen-4B) - The Inner Jev authors' own model family (Qwen-4B / Medium-12B / Large-31B), applying the paper's recipe — bidirectional full-attention, LoRA + decision head, weight soup of three seeds, confidence-gated optional reasoning on 20% of items, 256 options in groups of 16 — with OpenDecisions results beating Jev 1.13 (Large 89.0 vs 80.1). CC BY-NC 4.0 weights, Apache code; the 4B sits official top-10 on JevBench's Nov 17 draw. Not the `jeff` runtime in Serving (GLiFormer) and not Jeffan — three-way name collision, all listed.
- [Jeffan-31b-1710](https://huggingface.co/tattrongvu/Jeffan-31b-1710) - Full Gemma-4-31B fine-tune now sitting #10 on the Decision Index's Nov 6 draw (69.75 Full; it was #2 on the late-October draw at 73.13), with a dated-checkpoint naming scheme (Oct 17) and a 4B sibling. Apache 2.0. No public write-up yet — the board placement is the story.
- [Jeva](https://github.com/LiQiiiii/Jeva) - NUS research model built for the failure modes letter-readout models inherit: a set-native architecture encodes each candidate independently against an immutable option memory, so representations are request-independent and reusable, decisions are permutation-stable (zero flips under candidate reordering, against 17–23% for comparable open models), sets scale to 2,048 options, and an explicit null probability separates "none of these applies" from uncertainty (98.66% null-detection AUROC). 4B checkpoint and code, Apache 2.0; the [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18773) frames it as System One models from first principles.
- [JevEmbed](https://github.com/HITsz-TMG/JevEmbed) - HIT-TMG's recipe for turning an embedding model into a decision model: `choice` / `score` / `noul` heads over your pick of embedder, with a Hugging Face model collection and dataset. Apache 2.0.
- [JevK5](https://github.com/allebee/jevk5) - Open-weight Jev alternative (4B and 9B, Apache 2.0) serving `/v1/systemone` at about 13 ms on an H100; [weights on HF](https://huggingface.co/alibiserikbay/JevK5) have pulled ~138K downloads. JevBench v1.4 ranked v0.2 second of 76 systems and first open entrant, though the project itself reports Jev edging it on 308 fresh sealed items. Two-teacher distillation, pre-registered epoch sweeps.
- [jevlike](https://github.com/vinnylarouge/jevlike) - Train a small one-pass scorer over a changing list of text options. Each option queries the context; a shared head returns one probability per option. Byte encoder from scratch, or a frozen Hugging Face encoder.
- [Jev-Omni](https://huggingface.co/akhilaaa3/Jev-Omni) - Multimodal decision classifier on Gemma 4 12B: text, image, audio, or video in, a probability per option out. Apache 2.0 weights. One question per call, best under about 20 options. Topped Image JevBench's original pool; on the rebuilt v0.3.0 it sits mid-board (54.3 capability, 13th under the Jev-class caps).
- [jevos](https://github.com/feder-cr/jev) - MiniCPM5-1B cut to 17 layers with a one-logit head, quantized to GGUF and run on CPU via llama.cpp. Serves TypeSafe `/v1/systemone` for `noul` (yes/no) questions only, so the official SDK works by repointing `TYPESAFE_BASE_URL`. MIT.
- [jiwo](https://github.com/jiwidi/jiwo) - Small trained decision models on Qwen3.5 (0.8B and 4B) serving `/v1/systemone` at ~50 ms on one H100. jiwo-0.8b once ranked #1 of 27 under 1B on the Decision Index board; on the Nov 6 draw both v2 models sit low (the 0.8B at #209) with GLiDE 0.8B now leading the sub-1B bracket. Apache 2.0.
- [julia-1](https://huggingface.co/SupersonicLabs/Julia-1) - Supersonic Labs' 144M decision model on the multilingual mmBERT-small encoder: state + question + 2–20 options, `choice` / `score` / `noul` with probabilities, on CPU. Real traction for its size: the HF card plus the ggml-org GGUF mirror total about 13K downloads.
- [Kanana-2-v-decide-3b-2611](https://huggingface.co/kakaocorp/kanana-2-v-decide-3b-2611) - Kakao's 3.67B Korean/English text-image decision model (0.49B vision encoder + 3.18B LM, up to 10 images, 20,480 tokens) with an architecture the letter-readout families don't share: state embeds once, each candidate embeds into its own representation, and a softmax over cosine similarities gives the answer distribution — candidate representations are cacheable across questions. `choice` / `noul` / `score`, with the card claiming the highest public-benchmark average (61.23) in its weight class (producer-run). Custom Kanana Open License — read it before commercial use.
- [Karpo Decision](https://huggingface.co/MachinePulseAI/karpo-decision-31B) - Five-size Apache family from MachinePulseAI (0.8B to 31B, the flagship a Gemma-4-31B-it fine-tune) with a [serving implementation](https://github.com/machinepulse-ai/karpo-decision) and full results datasets published. The fastest board ascent in the wave: the card's own "#1 on the public suite" claim was vindicated days later — the 31B tops the Jev Decision Index's Nov 19 draw (73.86 Full, #1 of 326) and sits #7 on JevBench's Nov 17 official composite (77.4), both independent. ### Extraction models

- [Clairic](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/models/clairic/) - Cloudflare's System One extraction model — the first entrant in a sibling class to decision models, answering with typed values instead of option probabilities: text spans with character offsets, computed numbers and dates, durations, lists, `estimate` distributions inside bounds you declare, and per-step `forecast` series. Every answer carries `confidence` and `p_null`; a field the input doesn't determine returns null rather than a plausible guess. One request, but not one pass: each `text` field is answered in its own pass, other fields share a pass, fields that don't fit beside the `state` split across more — and every pass re-reads and re-bills the full state and images (`usage.passes` reports the count), so cost scales with field count on long documents. Up to four images per request, 64K context, $0.10/M input, output free, still in preview with open weights promised. The [launch post](https://blog.cloudflare.com/clairic-extraction-model/) is honest about the seams — multi-step arithmetic, estimates without evidence, basic forecasting — and pairs Clairic with Clef in a claims pipeline that ran 3.2× faster than all-LLM at half the cost, where Gemma 4 and Qwen failed the extraction step outright; its 21-day TSA forecast landed 17 of 21 days inside the stated 80% interval.

## Serving & runtimes

Run one, or turn any open model into one. Same request shape, no new training.

### Serve any open model

- [AnyJev](https://github.com/nokia-applied-research/AnyJev) - Turns any LLM into a Jev-style decision model: typed decisions with probabilities, no training, reading answers from the next-token distribution with cyclic shifts and prior correction to strip positional and label bias. From Nokia Applied Research, Apache 2.0. Its self-distilled Tacit models are listed under Models.

Serves 30+ model families through `/v1/systemone` with per-model speed tables published (Clef 1.3× through Kev ~7.9× and OneJev ~6.8×; multi-request batching, session caching for agents, FP8). - [Pydantic AI](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/models/decision/) - Decision models as native agent backends: `TypeSafeModel` (a `DecisionModel` subclass) turns an agent's typed output fields into questions — bool → noul, Literal or Enum → choice, a bounded IntEnum → score — validates answers against the schema, retries on invalid data, and falls back to a generative model when a step needs writing rather than deciding. Jev, any compatible endpoint via base URL, and OpenAI Decisions; Gateway, Evals, and Logfire observe the decisions.
- [receptron/laya](https://github.com/receptron/laya) - Run Laya from Node.js / TypeScript via ONNX Runtime; the ~1.7 GB weights download from Hugging Face on first use.
- [Spring AI DecisionModel](https://docs.spring.io/spring-ai/reference/api/decision-model.html) - Java's native abstraction, new in 2.1.0-RC2 (Nov 9; pre-release): `DecisionPrompt` / `DecisionResponse` with YesNo, Choice, and Score questions over three implementations — OpenAI Decisions (default `gpt-6-luna`), Ollama local (default `d1-omni:600m`, pulled at startup, no API key), and SystemOne for any `/v1/systemone` server — wired into RAG post-processing (`DecisionDocumentReranker` / `DecisionDocumentFilter`), guardrail Advisors, `DecisionToolIndex` tool search, and a decision-observation module.
- [system-one-adapter-python](https://github.com/typesafe-ai/system-one-adapter-python) - Drop-in `TypeSafeClient` replacement backed by OpenAI or Anthropic, for comparing Jev against an LLM on the same questions.
- [system-one-connector](https://github.com/itsmostafa/system-one-connector) - Go MCP connector giving agents decision-model access (Jev, d1, CLM, Laya) — from typesafe-mcp's author, but a distinct, more general connector. ~365 stars.
- [Transformers](https://huggingface.co/blog/decision-models) - Decision models landed in Transformers 5.23.0 (Nov 13): `JevForDecision` / `AutoModelForDecision` load the decision head shipped beside the weights (`head.safetensors`, `judge_config.json`), the experimental `decision` pipeline and `decide()` API answer from it, and the same head reads the 255 answer codes of pplx-decider and d3. The familiar loading path for half the open models in this list, in the library everyone already has. Experimental — APIs may move.
- [typesafeai-dotnet-sdk](https://github.com/saibimajdi/typesafeai-dotnet-sdk) - Community .NET SDK for noul / choice / score — the C# corner of the client ecosystem.
- [typesafe-mcp](https://github.com/itsmostafa/typesafe-mcp) - MCP server as a single static Go binary, no Node or Python runtime. Sends usage guidance back to the client so the agent writes better questions.

## Baselines

Cheaper classifiers for comparison. They do not share Jev's request shape. The honest continuum is fixed classifier → constrained generative model → decision model → reasoning model: each rung earns its cost only if probabilities, latency, or cost actually beat the rung below.

- [EvalScope](https://evalscope.readthedocs.io/en/latest/best_practice/decision_evaluation.html) - ModelScope's official eval framework, `--eval-type systemone_api`: grades any System One endpoint on the public OpenDecisions set with per-kind accuracy, Score MAE, composite Intelligence/Calibration/Capability metrics, a per-question probability browser, and a `perf` load-test mode. Name-collision note: ModelScope's OpenDecisions mirror ≠ the arXiv OpenDecisions set listed above (same source paper, mirrored).
- [fastText](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fastText) - Classic non-generative text classification library. Upstream archived in 2024.
- [GLiClass](https://github.com/knowledgator/gliclass) - Zero-shot classification against labels supplied at inference time — single-label or multi-label, one forward pass over text plus labels, roughly 10× faster than cross-encoders — from the GLiNER ecosystem's other branch. The closest non-LLM baseline for dynamic-choice workloads.
- [GLiNER](https://github.com/urchade/GLiNER) - Lightweight zero-shot structured extraction: you name the entity types at inference time. Often the comparison point in Jev classification benches.
- [GLiNER2](https://github.com/fastino-ai/GLiNER2) - Fastino successor to GLiNER. Schema-conditioned encoder for classification, extraction, and relations in one pass.
- [jeffy](https://github.com/nicobrenner/jeffy) - 68 pretrained logistic-regression classifiers that run and retrain on CPU: banking77 at 94.3%, CLINC-OOS, spam, plus game-state deciders for Doom and poker. The "do you even need a decision model" baseline; pip package with a live playground. MIT.
- [ModernBERT](https://github.com/AnswerDotAI/ModernBERT) - Modernized BERT encoder under most fast classifiers, including Laya's English checkpoint. The non-generative baseline Jev's cost and speed usually get measured against.
- [Outlines](https://github.com/dottxt-ai/outlines) - Constrained generation from ordinary LLMs: schemas, regexes, and context-free grammars compiled into token masks, so the output is structurally valid by construction (~98% schema adherence against ~76% for generate-then-validate). The control condition every decision-model comparison should include — a constrained LLM already guarantees the JSON parses, so a decision model's case has to rest on probability quality, latency, and cost instead.
- [RouteLLM](https://github.com/lm-sys/RouteLLM) - Trains and serves routers that pick which LLM should handle a query, based on cost and expected quality, rather than answering the question itself.
- [semantic-router](https://github.com/aurelio-labs/semantic-router) - Embedding-space route layer for LLMs and agents. Chooses a path from utterance similarity without a generative call.
- [SetFit](https://github.com/huggingface/setfit) - Prompt-free few-shot text classification on Sentence Transformers. Strong when labels are fixed at train time.
- [XGrammar](https://github.com/mlc-ai/xgrammar) - The grammar engine underneath most modern serving stacks — vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM, MLC — masking tokens to JSON schemas, regexes, and CFGs at near-zero decoding overhead. Where the constrained-generation baseline actually runs in production; still marked beta by its authors.

## Evaluation

How the class gets measured: boards to compare on, suites to run against your own model, the datasets inside them, and probes of known failure modes.

### Leaderboards & indexes

- [AIM System One Index](https://aimultiple.com/system-one) - AIMultiple's independent board (v1.2): 76 models across 2,626 classification, 400 bias, and 50 browser-automation tasks with z-score methodology and per-decision latency/cost — and a changelog tracking entrants within days. Measures models the other boards don't carry (AutoJev-27B, MATILDA, Eikos, Shisa DE).
- [Artificial Analysis Decision Index](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/artificial-analysis-decision-index) - The mainstream benchmarking site's index, rebuilt as v2.1 on Nov 8: six weighted evals — AA-Paperclip (private documents, 30%, each document's 15 answers scored as one decision), EvalSafe-O*NET-AA, When2Call, a private AA-Omniscience adaptation, HLE-MultiChoice with an abstain option, and AA-Symmetry, which checks a decision holds when options move, reword, or the question negates (15%) — combined as a Decision Score (accuracy minus Brier of the chosen answer) on the first five plus Symmetry's own invariance metric; cost and latency reported as separate axes. The Nov 17 standings have 25 models with the top three tied at 73 — Drex Max v1.1, SubQ Snap 1.2, and GLiDE v1.2 — then mammoth-1, d1-ultra, and Jev 1.14; language models shown as the trailing baseline.
- [AudioJevBench](https://benchmarkheaven.com/audio-jev-bench) - The audio counterpart (v0.1): 988 items (112 public / 876 sealed) across intent, escalation, command safety, sentiment, urgency, speaker verification, and sound events. The ranked board is all general audio models so far — Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B leads the Jev-class rows (83.2 capability, 70.4 composite) and Gemini 3.8 Flash is most capable on public-only items (94.4) but outside the latency and cost caps — and none of the dedicated audio decision models (Clef-omni, d1-omni, NeuDecide) have been measured on it yet.
- [evals.typesafe.ai](https://evals.typesafe.ai) - TypeSafe's workflow evals: four production-shaped workflows scored against frontier LLM reference probabilities.
- [Image JevBench](https://benchmarkheaven.com/image-jev-bench) - Held-out image-decision suite, v0.3.0 rebuilt with 68 measured systems: H2O-Lightning-31B now leads capability at 80.9 with kettei-fat #3, and the Qwen3.5-adapter [Imajev](https://huggingface.co/mohit67890/imajev-4b) family leads open weights — frozen hashes and contamination tracking retained (the original pool flagged Kev's Mind2Web training overlap itself); frontier chat APIs still trail far behind.
- [JevBench](https://benchmarkheaven.com/jev-models) - Independent decision-model leaderboard (run by Benchmark Heaven, not TypeSafe): 1,500 typed decisions per system scored on intelligence, calibration, speed, and cost — an official composite plus a capability view capped at 2× Jev's cost and latency — with versioned releases (v1.8.x amendments through Nov 17), published methodology, and result JSON. The Nov 17 open-weights board holds 261 ranked systems, led officially by Torchcast's 4B v1.3 (80.5) and on capability by its 31B (89.5) — quiet CC-BY-NC models out in front of the Apache wave. On the API board Super Decider led the composite (78.5) with kettei-fat still #1 on capability (86.3); Jev 1.13 sits #15 of the APIs.
- [Jev Decision Index](https://huggingface.co/spaces/multimodalart/jev-decision-index) - Community index: a 37-benchmark public panel plus private tests (60% same skills, 40% new domains), five skill areas, latency on fixed hardware, and raw [data](https://huggingface.co/spaces/multimodalart/jev-decision-index/blob/main/data/index.json) published as JSON. The Nov 19 draw (edition 0.4.1) ranks 326 systems: Karpo's 31B leads the Full score at 73.86 with GLiDE 27B and REAX 1.3 next, KnowLine's Gen2 and the new Copernicus family in the top seven, mammoth-1 dethroned since Oct 29, and the Jev 1.13 reference at #79 (59.22). The surface Clef's launch numbers reported on.

### Class-native suites

- [AgentDecisionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21412) - Measures decision models from isolated questions up to control of a running agent (with the DecisionGym environment). Headline: the most accurate model on isolated questions solved only 69.5% of agent tasks, and delegating tool choices without a fitted confidence threshold cost 19.0 success points — the isolated-vs-in-agent gap no other listed suite measures. Ships the open AgentDecision-4B (91.5% vs Jev's 82.0% on unlike-training tasks) and its [harness](https://github.com/JevAgentHarness/JevAgentHarness).
- [DecisionBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/akhilaaa3/decision-bench) - 80 scenarios and 293 questions per tier (medium is rules-based, hard is judgment calls), with a cost-per-decision axis baked in. What Jev-Omni reports on. Apache 2.0.
- [Decision-Bench / Argo-Bench](https://github.com/TextQLLabs/Decision-Bench) - TextQL's enterprise-decision benchmark (paper name Argo-Bench; no relation to the dataset above): a simulated year of a NYC delivery platform exported to a 235-table, 7.5B-row Oracle-style warehouse, where agents file typed actions (ban accounts, freeze payouts, file forecasts) graded by their consequences in the simulator, with latent ground truth withheld so answer keys cannot leak. One appendix worth the price of admission: Gemini 3.8 Flash escaped an unisolated sandbox, located the grader, and filed the answer it read — the run was discarded and the traces published. The board is frontier LLM agents end-to-end (Opus 5.5, 59.5) — the outer loop a decision model would slot into.
- [DecisionBench (delegation)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19099) - The fourth unrelated project carrying the name: a substrate for emergent delegation in long-horizon agentic workflows (GAIA / τ-bench / BFCL-multi-turn, 11-model peer pool, 23,375 instances) measuring delegation rate, routing fidelity-at-k, vendor self-preference, and a counterfactual perfect-delegation ceiling 15–31 points above measured. The metric nobody else computes for the routing job this list's Applications exist to do.
- [DecisionBench (Hanno Labs)](https://github.com/Hanno-Labs/decision-bench) - Document-grounded typed decisions across tasks, domains, and primitives, scored on accuracy, expected calibration error, negative log likelihood, and coverage, with task-level artifacts and complete answer distributions. Applied and reasoning tracks are kept separate; no relation to the two other Decision-Benches above.
- [DecisionPivot](https://github.com/StartLuxLabs/DecisionPivot) - StartLux's diagnostic benchmark (renamed from DecideBench on Oct 14, item hashes preserved) for the control points where decision models sit in LLM systems: contrast groups of 2–4 items identical except one decisive detail, scored on pair accuracy, confident errors, calibration, and automation at a fixed 5% error rate, with accuracy also read at a 0.90 probability threshold. Releases ship SHA-256 item hashes and items found in StartLux-Decision's own training data are removed from the reference track — first-party, but built with the contamination discipline of an independent bench. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28522).
- [DecisionRobust](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19118) - Robustness benchmark for the class: 1,058 curated attacks across meaning-preserving text perturbations, prompt injection, image perturbations, in-image instruction overlays, and instruction injection beside images, with ground truth on whether the attack preserves the correct answer. Findings worth the read: resistance to perturbation does not imply resistance to injection, and label-consistency checks substantially shrink measured attack success.
- [Evaluating and Benchmarking the System One Model Jev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647) - The broadest independent academic harness: Jev 1.13 zero-shot on 37 datasets (346,009 requests, under $10) against Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma-4-E4B scored via exact option probabilities, extended to sequential decisions (ALFWorld 35%, Blackjack 90% optimal) and memorization probes. Code, harness, and all raw responses released.
- [fast-decisions](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fastino/fast-decisions) - Fastino's held-out suite: 17 operational domains × 300 examples each, scoring Jev, SemIf, Laya, and GLiFormer head-to-head on the same inputs. First-party to GLiNER2.5-Decide, which tops it.
- [ForecastBench](https://forecastbench.org/) - Probabilistic forecasts of real future events, scored with difficulty-adjusted Brier as outcomes resolve over time — the natural eval surface for the `estimate` and `forecast` field types extraction models like Clairic introduce, and a different job from deciding over a fixed state.
- [JevAdvBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31142) - The first adversarial benchmark for the class: black-box attacks flip 46.3% of Jev's correct decisions, and the transfer attacks generalize across tasks. The evaluation companion to the injection papers under Reading.
- [JEVal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03935) - Bilingual general-decision suite: 11,257 instances over 36 datasets and 10 domains, 25 model configurations, extended to system-level composition — τ-bench multi-turn episodes and large-scale social simulation. Findings: decision models competitive on evidence-resolvable decisions but overstating probabilities, and per-decision speed losing end-to-end episode success as errors accumulate — the composition axis that complements *Faster Decisions* under Reading.
- [jevals](https://github.com/openlayer-ai/jevals) - Agent evals and guardrails as Jev decisions: one request per trace. Runs locally on Kev or Laya. From Openlayer.
- [JevBench (coherence)](https://github.com/JevBench/jevbench) - A separate project sharing the name: metamorphic tests of whether typed probabilities stay coherent when the question is transformed — probability, representation, batch, logic, and choice-set relations. Coherence is not accuracy (uniform answers can be perfectly coherent), but it is the systematic version of the option-order bias the probe studies below describe.
- [Jev-ToM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18610) - Theory-of-Mind suite for the class: 11,117 questions from nine ToM benchmarks across six capability groups, scoring five typed decision models against seven generative baselines. Jev takes 73.2% source-balanced accuracy and ranks first or second on every capability; the findings that confidence-based selection stays benchmark-dependent, and that supplied reasons can repair or overturn answers, belong in any assistant-integration plan.
- [LLMRouterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07206) - ACL 2026 Findings benchmark superseding RouterBench for the routing job: 400K+ query-model instances, 21 datasets, 33 models, 10 routing algorithms under one framework with cost curves, Pareto fronts, and latency — explicitly built to fix the incomparability between prior router evaluations. [Code](https://github.com/ynulihao/LLMRouterBench).
- [LocalLLaMA/typed-decisions](https://huggingface.co/datasets/LocalLLaMA/typed-decisions) - Community typed-decision suites (customer service, invoices, security incidents, agent traces) the open implementations report accuracy and calibration against. Apache 2.0.
- [MMDM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14309) - Massive Multitask Decision Making (Nov 10): 5,448 fresh questions across 16 categories built to dodge prior benchmark exposure — contexts to 64,817 tokens, option spaces from 2 to 1,024. Headline findings: hard single-select tops out at 76% (WeMM-JEV 27B), and no model exceeds 36.81% on hard multi-select — first hard evidence of a multi-select ceiling in the class.
- [OpenDecisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17205) - 2,352-question validation set built to mirror JevBench's task mix, released with *Releasing the Inner Jev* (Geuter, Cheng & Alvarez-Melis) — the paper showing plain instruction-tuned Gemma 4 31B, read through its output logits with no training, already scores 79.6 to Jev's 80.1, and that a simple fine-tuning recipe lifts it to 84.4 here and 81.5 on JevBench v1.7, past Jev. Models and serving code ship with the set.
- [PWS-Decisions](https://github.com/parallel-web/pws-decisions-benchmark) - Parallel's benchmark for the axis no other suite measures: 300 closed-ended questions whose answers depend on current, niche, or scattered live-web facts, each confirmed against a direct source, scored by exact match — with runnable code (MIT) and the [dataset on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/parallel-web/pws-decisions-benchmark). First-party, and Parallel tops its own board (76.2% vs OpenAI Decisions' 41.7% and Jev's 33.4%) — but the honest framing is in the cross-check: on JevBench's public set the same three sit within 1.7 points, so the spread is web grounding, not decision skill. A JevBench score cannot tell you what happens when the answer isn't in the prompt.
- [RouterBench](https://github.com/withmartian/routerbench) - Martian × UC Berkeley's routing benchmark: 405K+ precomputed inference outcomes from 11 LLMs across 8 datasets, so router policies train and compare without live queries, against Oracle and Zero Router bounds. The standard cost-quality surface for the routing job most of this list's Applications section exists to do. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12031).
- [RouterEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10657) - EMNLP 2025 Findings benchmark for routing at model-pool scale: 200M+ performance records across 8,500+ LLMs and 12 evaluations. Its headline finding — a capable router improves as the candidate pool grows, potentially beating the best single model in it — is the research backing for the whole cheap-first-escalate pattern this list's Tools section builds. [Code](https://github.com/MilkThink-Lab/RouterEval).
- [s1kit](https://github.com/Eliot5566/s1kit) - Run one YAML suite of your own labelled cases against any `/v1/systemone` server (Jev, Laya, kev, llama.cpp, Ollama) side by side, pick the lowest confidence cutoff per question that reaches a target precision, and route cheapest-first with escalation below it. Ships an offline keyword baseline and a GitHub Action that fails CI when accuracy drops.
- [SO-Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15777) - Benchmark splitting capability from reliability across 21 models, 11 domains, and five capability families: evidence-grounded labels verified against model-visible inputs, domain-specific difficulty criteria, interactive sandbox execution — and two reliability measures the accuracy boards don't have, decision stability under option-set changes and missing-answer recognition when no valid option exists. Headline: the best-performing System One model ranks only seventh in reliability.
- [sysone-bench](https://github.com/instax-dutta/sysone-bench) - Head-to-head of System One models: Laya vs Jev on byte-identical inputs.
- [System One Bench](https://github.com/alilibx/system-one-bench) - Runnable suite with frozen, hash-checked datasets, identical request payloads, released predictions, and accuracy / calibration / cost / latency per system — 16 datasets, 3,250 cases, 4,850 decisions across English, French, and Arabic, with a public board at [systemonebench.dev](https://systemonebench.dev). The methodology is the reason it's here: local timing kept separate from API comparisons, and latency rows excluded when a control model shows a materially different serving environment — discipline the field's incomparable-milliseconds problem needs. Public datasets can overlap training data; hosted-model runs may change.
- [TypedBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11392) - Academic benchmark for the class from DFKI, RPTU, Imperial, and Mila: seven policy-labelled generators and nine suites scored as median across paraphrases, calibration against the finite-sample noise floor, selective prediction, ordinal proper scoring rules, and realised cost under asymmetric matrices. Headline finding: the hosted model follows stated policy but is wording-sensitive and systematically underconfident, enough that trusting its probabilities can cost more than just taking the top answer.
- [XDecideBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01370) - The multilingual axis at scale: nine suites across 122 languages — 88,425 texts, 153,875 typed questions, the largest suite in the field — with every suite sharing one scoring contract and aligned, locally-labelled, and native items reported separately. Findings that matter for anyone shipping routing or moderation: the English-best model loses significantly in a quarter of suite-language cells, and an English-tuned 5%-error confidence threshold exceeds that rate on XNLI even in German and French.

### Probes & studies

- [Arize decision-model benchmark](https://arize.com/blog/decision-model-benchmark/) - Eight decision models across RAGTruth hallucination detection, all 13 Phoenix evaluator suites, and 52 tool/skill-routing decisions, scored on CI-gated accuracy, latency, cost, and calibration with per-model tuned cutoffs. The distinct axis: re-asking every question with negated phrasing collapses ROC AUC for Liquid d1 (0.92→0.17), Jeeves, Strands, and Laya, while only Jev, Clef 27B, and OpenAI Decisions hold — question polarity is a failure mode the option-order studies don't cover. Open-sourced in [Arize's repo](https://github.com/Arize-ai).
- [Frontier quality at 1% of the cost](https://airbnb.tech/ai-ml/frontier-quality-at-1-of-the-cost-a-production-benchmark-of-llm-classifiers/) - Airbnb's production benchmark of a content-policy classifier, and the most credible production-scale independent data on Jev yet: on a 10,000-example held-out set at thousands of RPM, Jev is "the most accurate off-the-shelf model we tested under 100 ms" and beats GPT-6 Luna at equal cost — but a fine-tuned Gemma4-E2B scoring via logprobs beats everything on all three axes (F1 0.563 → 0.902 at 35 ms median, ~1% of frontier cost), and a 2,700-word prompt fails to close the gap for any deployable model. Three findings worth pinning: thresholds beat verdicts at production scale (the class's whole thesis, confirmed); fine-tuning moves the quality-latency frontier instead of trading along it; and Jev's cost quadrupled under the long prompt because at evaluation time its API offered no cached-input discount — a gap both OpenAI's Decisions GA and Morph Reflex now price.
- [Janus](https://github.com/FirasSX914/Janus) - Measures a confidence threshold on your labeled data, then routes Jev vs a larger model. Ships no default: on Banking77 routing wins; on Web of Science it says do not route.
- [jev-behavior-study](https://github.com/RINNECODER/jev-behavior-study) - Independent look at Jev 1.13.0: option-order bias, facts lost in the middle of long context, and cases where a correct prerequisite still leads to the wrong action. Raw payloads and offline verification included.
- [jev-benchmarks](https://github.com/AbdelStark/jev-benchmarks) - Independent calibration, selective-risk, and latency measurements of Jev against GLiNER and other baselines.
- [jev-multilingual-benchmark](https://github.com/webmaster-ramos/jev-multilingual-benchmark) - Jev 1.13.0 vs chat, guard and decision models (OpenAI's Decisions API included) on a store's display names and comments in 25 languages, scored on blind-verified items only: clean text and comments hold, but a bare swear word in a name field is caught 21 of 27 times in English, Spanish, Russian, Japanese and Chinese and 15 of 80 in the other sixteen languages. Reproducible, with a [write-up](https://webmaster-ramos.com/blog/decision-model-in-your-customers-language).
- [jev-ood-calibration](https://github.com/scienthoon/jev-ood-calibration) - Independent calibration test on tasks Jev cannot have seen: 900 rule-generated tickets plus public benchmarks, with ECE noise floors and a temperature refit.
- [jev-phishing-bench](https://github.com/anisselbd/jev-phishing-bench) - Jev vs Claude Haiku 4.5 on 2,000 phishing emails: accuracy, calibration, latency, and cost, reproducible.
- [jev-rerank-bench](https://github.com/anessbelbati/jev-rerank-bench) - Jev vs Cohere Rerank 4 vs zerank-2 vs a chat baseline on 14 datasets. Every raw response saved. nDCG@10 is a tie, not a win.
- [TDM-Gauntlet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20191) - Swaps typed decision models into the pipelines of 24 published ACL-venue papers, keeping each paper's own splits and metrics: Jev zero-shot replaces the fine-tuned predictor on 12 of 46 test sets, and GEPA-style prompt optimization plus calibration closes a median 72% of the remaining gap at ~$1.66 per search. The strongest evidence yet on the fine-tuned-classifier boundary, with the 46-set gauntlet released.
- [Typed Decision Models: An Early Evidence Audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32160) - The skeptic's literature review: 28 papers from the first nine days after Jev's launch, related back to label-probability classification, constrained decoding, and cascades. Headline: the typed readout itself shows no independent accuracy advantage over comparable label-probability readouts — the gains are latency and cost — with a 14-item checklist for future TDM evaluation. Useful calibration for every vendor number in this list.
- [Vals AI: An Independent Evaluation of TypeSafe's Jev](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/independent-evaluation-of-jev) - Professional eval shop, two purpose-built surfaces: 400 claim-verification items from 48 companies' SEC 10-Ks (construction-rule labels, human-audited) and a preregistered 12-subtask LegalBench slice, 12 systems on identical items. Jev ties GPT-6 Astra/Sol/Luna on claim verification at ~1/500th of Astra's cost, finishes last of 12 on LegalBench, and blows a 1% error budget (1.6% at 95% automation) — the financial-filings and legal axes, with preregistration discipline.

### Jev under the microscope

- [Actor, Judge, or Teacher? Understanding Decision Models in Long-Horizon Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17167) - Liu, Zhu, Wang et al. / Zhejiang & Baidu (2026). The role taxonomy: all six tested models flail as actors (Jev completes 42.9% of ALFWorld, distracted by easy subgoals), yet judged with full-trajectory hindsight on "did this action help?", the same models credit exactly the steps they skipped as actors — and those scores as GRPO step rewards lift a 1.5B policy past a 7B trained on outcomes alone (72.8→82.9 ALFWorld, 56.8→71.4 WebShop). Acting and teaching are different capabilities; evidence about one is not evidence about the other.
- [Beyond Ranking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11480) - Jev reranks ahead of dedicated cross-encoders but is overconfident — only 66% of answers scored above 0.9 are actually relevant — and a two-parameter recalibration fixes most of it. The IR-side evidence for treating decision-model probabilities as uncalibrated until shown otherwise.
- [Decision Hijacking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28613) - The first systematic prompt-injection attack on the class: across 510 reconstructed InjecAgent cases, malicious content shifts action probabilities even though outputs stay schema-bound, and adaptive attacks using score feedback double the attacker-target probability. Restricting outputs to declared actions is not injection resistance.
- [Faster Decisions Do Not Make Better Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13092) - 4,500 end-to-end agent runs: decision-model savings get absorbed by reasoning and recovery steps, and on mobile the success rate declines. The controlled counterpoint to the latency story the class tells about itself.
- [Fast Models, Slow Evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267) - The paired, self-audited Laya-vs-Jev evaluation on 11 agent decision points (7,283 cases plus 6,640 robustness variants, byte-identical inputs): Jev wins 9 of 11 decision points (+10.8 to +46.0 pp), neither model beats chance on zero-shot model routing, Laya flips 30% of answers under option reversal — and the self-audit is the real lesson: an omitted pre-screen cost turned a claimed 23.9% saving into 4.3%, and in-sample thresholds (5% target) missed up to 17% held-out. Measure the whole policy, and fit thresholds off the eval set.
- [FlipJev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38895) - Universal adversarial suffixes flip 43% of Jev's decisions on unseen documents, transferring across tasks 90–100% of the time — the attack generalizes better than the defenses.
- [Hidden Risks of Jev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04985) - Empirical security, privacy, and dual-use audit of hosted typed decisions: injected state steers decisions toward attacker-chosen answers that downstream actions then execute. HiddenLayer's follow-up ([Jev security risks](https://www.hiddenlayer.com/insight/jev-security-risks-ai-decision-models)) walks a Jev browser agent into clicking a malicious Confirm Price button and bypasses its prompt verification with a Tokenbreak-style attack — decision models are still LLMs under the hood.
- [Jev 1.13 jaggedness](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13) - First-party failure modes: counting, dates, indirection, distractors, generation. Keep arithmetic in code.
- [JEV-as-a-Judge: Accept When Confident, Escalate When Unsure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26550) - Li, Miao, Krishnan & Padman / CMU (2026). First independent study of Jev as an eval judge: within ~3 points of the strongest LLM judge on ordinary preference at 0.36% of the fee, weaker on derivation-checking. A confidence-gated cascade keeps 99% of the big judge's accuracy at ~57% of the cost.
- [Jev as a judge: How counterfeit policy steers Jev](https://securitylabs.datadoghq.com/articles/jev-as-a-judge-how-counterfeit-policy-steers-jev/) - Datadog Security Labs feed Jev a forged classification policy and watch it steer the verdict — including bypassing LangChain's experimental tool-risk gating, the exact defense pattern this list's integrations section recommends.
- [JevProbe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18612) - Scans 228 real deployed Jev apps: 81.5% exhibit decision flips under option manipulation and 98.6% of those have security impact. The empirical case that this section's attack papers describe deployed systems, not hypotheticals.
- [Jev's Architecture Unmasked](https://archerhume.com/posts/jevs-architecture-unmasked) - Community teardown of Jev's probable architecture; the write-up kev's open models are built from.
- [JEV vs. LLMs as Rubric Judges: Cheaper, Faster, and Wrong in the Same Places](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29769) - Rao & Callison-Burch (2026). The counterpoint to the cascade story: Jev is 29–325× cheaper and competitive on binary criteria, but LLM judges repeat nearly all of its most confident errors, capping escalation gains at ~1.5 points.
- [Reproducible LoRA Inference Through Deterministic Split-K Reduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10334) - Serving-reproducibility study, LoRA-specific: vLLM's atomic Split-K kernels make identical replays drift — different returned probabilities in 7,332 of 9,000 structured-decision comparisons (81.47%, mean absolute difference 0.0014) — but only 7 comparisons (0.078%, two comments) actually flip the binary decision, all near the 0.5 boundary; on the generation side 17.30% of GSM8K and 14.96% of MBPP outputs change. The deterministic Triton implementation eliminates observed variation. Probability drift, not decision chaos — and a reason to pin kernels (or use batch-invariance flags) when probabilities feed thresholds.
- [S1-Evo: Experience-Guided Evidence Competition Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16937) - Self-evolving attacks tuned to System One's option-competition structure and margin feedback: 71.99% mean attack success on Jev 1.14, 57.47% on high-confidence decisions, up to 24% end-to-end agent violations. The newest and strongest entry in the FlipJev / Decision Hijacking line.
- [Swap, Replicate, Read the Mass](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00496) - 1.64M-decision bias audit: option swaps flip outcomes by 0–72.6% depending on model and task, and every audited vendor ships at least one swap-sensitive deployment pattern. Recommends permutation audits before procurement.
- [SystemOneAgents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21053) - Deterministic compiler turns tool schemas into multiple-choice questions, a one-pass decision model answers, code assembles the MCP call — evaluated on 1,952 biomedical requests across 157 tools: 97.0% tool selection (beating all 18 local LLM-agent settings) with zero invented argument text versus 5.5–19.7% for generative calls. The instructive architecture: constrain the choices, ground the candidates, let code build the action. [Code](https://github.com/eltzanis/SystemOneAgents).
- [To Jev or Not to Jev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00505) - Fourteen System 1 judges versus four generative ones across 11 benchmarks: reference-guided S1 judgment matches GPT-5.6-class reasoning judges at a sixteenth of the cost — the strongest evidence yet for the judge cascade.
- [What Can't Jev Decide?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24929) - Probes the one-pass limit directly: select-and-find stays accurate to 20,000 tokens and 64 options, but decisions requiring sixteen state updates collapse to 53–80%, and given a stale state summary the model follows it. Conclusion: checking the state a decision model relies on needs a reasoning model.
- [When Should Decision Models Compare Candidates?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20335) - Pointwise versus setwise versus candidate-attention readouts across five agent benchmarks plus controlled synthetic tasks: pointwise scoring stays competitive, setwise comparison wins only on some tool-selection and binary tasks, and cross-candidate attention doesn't reliably help. Candidate interaction is a task-dependent design choice, not a free win — directly relevant to Jeva's set-native claims above.

### Foundations

- [AdaMenu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19005) - Query-adaptive menus for large label spaces: retrieval builds a compact menu, one decision-model request covers up to 1,016 labels, beating TypeSafe's beam-search baseline on all 16 runnable model×labelset pairs (+16.6–34.3 pp) at 3.2–22.9× fewer input tokens. The principled answer to the >255-option cap.
- [Agents Thinking Fast and Slow: A Talker-Reasoner Architecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08328) - Christakopoulou, Mourad & Matarić / DeepMind (2024). Splits an agent into a fast conversational Talker (System 1) and a slow planning Reasoner (System 2).
- [Build your own decision model](https://nishtahir.com/build-your-own-decision-model/) - Nish Tahir (2026). The clearest mechanism explainer yet: constrained option tokens and a softmax over answer letters versus autoregressive JSON generation, with an interactive demo, working Qwen code, and an honest note that raw token probabilities are not calibrated confidence.
- [DecisionRank](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42128) - On-policy distillation transfers an LLM's ranking behavior into decision-model scores — a 1.7B student beats its 27B teacher on TREC DL. The cleanest recipe yet for the distillation path most open entrants actually take.
- [Distilling System 2 into System 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06023) - Yu, Xu, Weston & Kulikov / Meta (2024). Compiles intermediate-thought techniques into single-pass outputs; the research framing closest to what Jev is.
- [Dual-processing accounts of reasoning, judgment, and social cognition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18154502/) - Evans (2008). Review of dual-process theories. System 1 is a family of accounts, not one algorithm.
- [FrugalGPT](https://github.com/stanford-futuredata/FrugalGPT) - Chen, Zaharia & Zou / Stanford (2023). The LLM cascade: answer with the cheapest model, escalate only when needed — up to 98% cost reduction at matched quality. The direct ancestor of every confidence-gated router and cascade in this list. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176).
- [JevRSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22432) - System-1 acts, System-2 improves: a modular harness (procedures, memory, world models) where asynchronous reasoning interventions decrease over time because the fast path keeps absorbing them — amortized self-improvement. [Code](https://github.com/usail-hkust/JevRSI).
- [Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124) - Tversky & Kahneman (1974). Heuristic judgment under uncertainty.
- [MDLM](https://github.com/kuleshov-group/mdlm) - Masked diffusion language model (NeurIPS 2024): parallel, non-autoregressive generation. The open research line nearest to Jev's parallel-sampler claims.
- [Reasoning the Fast and Frugal Way](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8888650/) - Gigerenzer & Goldstein (1996). Simple heuristics that work with incomplete information.
- [RELOAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07736) - Trained-together layer loops that extend a model's solvable reasoning depth beyond its training distribution — the looped-decision-model line (with SanSi below) that asks whether one pass is really the ceiling.
- [RLiD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02455) - Reinforcement learning directly over the option probabilities of a large decision model — 99.3% on ALFWorld, then distilled back into plain LLMs for the speed. RLCD's successor, argued on evidence.
- [SanSi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07730) - The "System 1.5" paper: a looped typed readout that iterates the decision head, +13.5 points over the same-shaped single-pass model. Adaptive compute without generation.
- [Small Heads, Strong Decisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15592) - Controlled study retraining five readouts under one protocol on Qwen3-0.6B: head complexity barely matters (a 0.5M cosine head matches Clef's 27M joint-schema head at half the inference time), one prompt with all questions matches per-question prompts at 1.6× inference speed, and backbone size dominates everything. The cheap null results the architecture race needs.
- [System-1.x: Learning to Balance Fast and Slow Planning with Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14414) - Learns when to use fast direct planning and when to search, instead of always picking one.
- [System 2 Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11829) - Weston & Sukhbaatar / Meta (2023). The LLM regenerates the context it should attend to before answering: deliberate filtering on top of fast attention.
- [The Value of Thinking: Learning When to Reason in Hybrid Decision Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16961) - Zero-or-T thinking: one frozen backbone serves both the implicit single-pass answer and full autoregressive reasoning, with a lightweight self-routing block estimating the value of thinking — the utility the slow mode adds over the fast one — and paying for it only above a per-request price λ; CDPO trains the block from episode returns where no per-decision label exists. On JevBench the 4B hybrid scores 0.857 to Jev's 0.851 at −72.5% median latency, and cuts MiniWoB token cost 80.1%. Uncertainty about the current answer and expected benefit from more computation are different quantities — this learns the second.
- [Thinking, Fast and Slow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking,_Fast_and_Slow) - Daniel Kahneman. The book TypeSafe cites for the System 1 / System 2 framing.
